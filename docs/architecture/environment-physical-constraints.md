# Environment and physical constraints

**Normative.** Objective reality is physically constrained. Locations, topology,
containment, objects, resources, environmental conditions, hazards, damage, and
affordances are objective state. They advance through committed events and
simulation time whether or not any observer is present.

Spatial relations are distinct: containment, adjacency, visibility, reachability,
and traversability must not be inferred from one another. A movement proposal
becomes a position change only after the spatial executor validates the route,
access, capacity, and committed outcome. Inventory is containment and custody;
ownership, reservation, allocation, and consumption remain separate records.

Environmental conditions may propagate or decay. Hazards may expose unaware
agents when their objective position and protection satisfy the exposure rule.
Damage changes affordances only through a committed material effect; repair
requires a committed action and its required objective material path. Scheduled
topology changes resolve at simulation time and are retained in replay history.

Planning, belief, memory, narration, relationships, and communication may inform
which action an agent proposes. They do not move an entity, consume a resource,
alter a hazard, repair damage, or rewrite topology. Environmental events may
create durable evidence; observers gain knowledge only when a valid perception or
evidence path subsequently reaches them.

The reference fixtures are deliberately narrow: `environment-spatial` exercises
movement and containment, `environment-resources` exercises conservation and
custody, `environment-model` exercises passive environmental evolution, and
`environment-integration` composes those results with time, agency, evidence,
memory, trust, communication, and planning.
